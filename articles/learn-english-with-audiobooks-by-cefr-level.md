# How to Learn English with Audiobooks by CEFR Level

*By [Dobi Apps](https://dobiapps.com) · [← All guides](../README.md#guides-in-this-repository)*

Audiobooks work best for learning English when the book matches your level.
If the text is too hard, you stop understanding and start guessing. If it is
too easy, you learn very little. The CEFR scale (A1 to C2) gives you a
simple way to find the right level.

## Step 1: Find your level

Take a free CEFR test or assess yourself against the level descriptions. The
[CEFR level guide](https://dobiapps.com/blog/find-your-cefr-english-level)
on the Dobi Apps blog explains both methods.

| Level | What you can listen to comfortably |
| --- | --- |
| A1–A2 | Short, simple stories with everyday words, read slowly |
| B1–B2 | Graded novels and non-fiction with a clear storyline |
| C1–C2 | Unabridged books, interviews and fast natural speech |

## Step 2: Check the 98% rule

A useful rule of thumb from research on comprehensible input: you should know
about **98% of the words** on a page. Read one page of the transcript. If more
than 2 or 3 words in every 100 are new, go down a level. More on this in
[Comprehensible Input: Find Your Right English Level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/comprehensible-input-english-reading-level).

## Step 3: Read while you listen

Listening and reading the same text at the same time links the sound of each
word to its spelling. The
[Audiobook app](https://dobiapps.com/audiobook) highlights the transcript as
the narrator reads, so you never lose your place. For the evidence, see
[Does Reading While Listening to Audiobooks Improve English?](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency).

## Step 4: Adjust the speed and then shadow

- Start a little slower than normal if you miss a lot of words, then speed up
  as you improve. See
  [Does Audiobook Playback Speed Affect English Comprehension?](https://dobiapps.com/blog/audiobook-listening-speed-english-comprehension)
- Once a chapter feels easy, replay it and speak along with the narrator
  (shadowing). See
  [Does Shadowing Audiobooks Improve English Pronunciation?](https://dobiapps.com/blog/shadowing-audiobooks-english-pronunciation)

## Step 5: Save and review words

Tap new words, save them, and review them over the following days instead of
all at once. Spaced review is what makes vocabulary stick:
[How Spaced Repetition Builds English Vocabulary Retention](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary).
