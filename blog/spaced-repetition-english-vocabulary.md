# How Spaced Repetition Builds English Vocabulary Retention

*By [The Dobi Apps Team](https://dobiapps.com/authors/editorial) · Published 2026-09-10 · Updated 2026-09-13 · Originally published at [dobiapps.com](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary)*

> **Spaced repetition beats cramming for English vocabulary. A 2022 meta-analysis of 48 experiments found a medium-to-large effect on retention over massed study.**

**In short:** Spaced repetition builds English vocabulary retention by reviewing a word at increasing intervals instead of studying it repeatedly in one sitting. A 2022 meta-analysis of 48 experiments and 3,411 learners found spaced practice produces a medium-to-large effect on second language learning, and a classroom study found a spaced repetition tool significantly outperformed a no-treatment control group.

## What Is Spaced Repetition and Why Does It Work for English Vocabulary?

Spaced repetition is a study method that reviews a word at increasing
intervals, a day, then three days, then a week, then a month, rather than
studying it several times back to back and moving on. The underlying
"spacing effect" was first documented by Hermann Ebbinghaus in 1885: memory
retention is stronger when learning is spread across multiple short
sessions than when it is crammed into one long session, a pattern
Ebbinghaus paired with the "forgetting curve," the observation that newly
learned information decays quickly at first and then more slowly over
time.

For an English learner, this means a new word needs a review scheduled
while it is still fresh enough to recall with effort, not so soon that
recall is trivial and not so late that the word has already been
forgotten. Flashcard-based tools implement this with systems such as the
Leitner box, developed by Sebastian Leitner in 1972, which sorts cards into
compartments reviewed on increasingly long timers and moves a card back to
the first compartment whenever it is answered incorrectly ([Lafleur &
Kanazawa,
2024](https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/download/87/396/1359)).

| Leitner box | Review interval |
| --- | --- |
| Box 1 | 1 day |
| Box 2 | 3 days |
| Box 3 | 1 week |
| Box 4 | 1 month |
| Box 5 | 3 months |

A card answered correctly moves up a box and is reviewed less often; a card
answered incorrectly drops back to box 1, so study time concentrates on the
words a learner has not yet retained rather than the ones they already
know. This is a deliberate second step after a word first turns up in
context, whether that context is [a story read at the right
level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) or [an audiobook a learner
listened to while reading
along](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency).

## How Much Does Spaced Repetition Improve English Vocabulary Retention?

A 2022 meta-analysis by Soyeon Kate Kim and Stuart Webb, published in the
journal Language Learning, pooled 98 effect sizes from 48 experiments
covering 3,411 second language learners. Louis Lafleur and Yu Kanazawa
summarize its conclusions in a 2024 study in the Computer Assisted Language
Learning Electronic Journal: spacing "improves second language learning on
a medium to large effect size scale," shorter spacing intervals perform
about as well as longer ones on an immediate test but fall behind on a
delayed test, and equal and expanding spacing schedules produce
statistically similar results ([Lafleur & Kanazawa,
2024](https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/download/87/396/1359)).

> **48 experiments** — second language learning experiments, 3,411 learners total, analyzed in a 2022 meta-analysis finding a medium-to-large effect of spaced over massed practice
>
> *Source: [Kim & Webb, 2022, reported in Lafleur & Kanazawa, 2024](https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/download/87/396/1359)*

The practical takeaway is not that a learner must calculate an optimal
schedule. It is that any spacing beats none: reviewing a new English word
tomorrow and again next week outperforms reviewing it five times today and
never again, regardless of which specific spacing pattern a tool uses, the
same logic that makes reading [a little every day more effective than one
long weekly session](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) for building
vocabulary through stories.

## Does It Matter Whether Review Intervals Are Equal or Expanding?

Researchers have split on whether steadily increasing intervals
("expanding spacing": a day, then a week, then a month) beat evenly spaced
ones ("equal spacing": every four days, say) for vocabulary retention.
Balota, Duchek, and Logan's review found massed study clearly performed
worst but no significant difference between equal and expanding patterns.
Schuetze and Weimer-Stuckmann's comparative study found equal spacing
reached 83% retention against 59% for expanding spacing after a nine-month
delay. Nakata's 2015 study found the opposite: expanding spacing showed a
small but significant edge, a 4.6% score increase, over equal spacing.

Kim and Webb's 2022 meta-analysis, pooling across these conflicting
individual studies, found equal and expanding spacing statistically similar
overall, with variability across studies better explained by differences in
methodology, such as number of learning sessions and feedback timing, than
by the spacing pattern itself ([Lafleur & Kanazawa,
2024](https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/download/87/396/1359)).
For a learner choosing a vocabulary app, this means the specific algorithm
matters less than simply using one consistently instead of restudying a
word repeatedly in a single sitting.

## What Did a Classroom Study Using Spaced Repetition Software Find?

Lafleur and Kanazawa's own 2024 study, published in the Computer Assisted
Language Learning Electronic Journal, enrolled 74 Japanese university EFL
learners over two academic semesters, 24 weeks total, studying 963 words
from the New Academic Word List through software the authors built that
combined spaced repetition with "interleaving," rotating between different
aspects of a word (its meaning, its form, and its use in a sentence) rather
than drilling one aspect repeatedly.

> “"The treatment group significantly outperformed the control group in total score gains (p = .002, r = .360)."”
>
> — [Lafleur & Kanazawa, 2024](https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/download/87/396/1359)

Pretest scores were uneven across the three aspects of word knowledge the
study measured: 24.34% for meaning, 20.53% for form, and 13.16% for use.
Post-test gains came back far more even, +16.71% for meaning, +15.39% for
form, and +13.16% for use, which the authors attribute to the software's
interleaving algorithm deliberately rotating study time toward the aspects
of a word a learner had not yet mastered, the same underlying idea as a
Leitner box moving a poorly known card back to a shorter review interval.

## How Should You Build a Spaced Repetition Habit for English Vocabulary?

Start with words that already have context attached, new vocabulary from a
story or an audiobook a learner has read rather than an arbitrary frequency
list, since Lafleur and Kanazawa's study paired every word with a
contextualized sentence, audio, and a translation rather than the word in
isolation. [Extensive reading is what supplies that
context](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) in the first place, and
[reading while listening to an
audiobook](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency) supplies the
pronunciation alongside it, before a word ever reaches a review schedule.
Spacing out review of an individual word like this is a different mechanism
from rereading a whole passage until it stops requiring effort, the fluency
method covered in [does rereading a book build English
fluency](https://dobiapps.com/blog/repeated-reading-english-fluency): one schedules single words
over days and weeks, the other repeats a full passage within one sitting to
build automaticity rather than long-term retention.

Pick a tool, such as [Anki's spaced repetition
scheduling](https://dobiapps.com/blog/top-5-apps-for-learning-english), or a paper Leitner box,
that automatically pushes a correctly recalled word
to a longer interval and drops a missed word back to a short one, rather
than reviewing every word on the same fixed schedule regardless of how well
a learner already knows it. Review daily in
short sessions instead of one long weekly session: the studies above
measured spacing in days and weeks, not in single cramming sessions, and a
missed day is far less costly than treating the whole system as optional
once motivation dips. Turning that daily review into an actual habit,
rather than a task a learner has to remember, is covered in [how to build an
English learning habit that lasts](https://dobiapps.com/blog/building-daily-english-learning-habit).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is spaced repetition better than flashcard apps used without a schedule?

It is the schedule that does the work. A flashcard is just the format; spaced repetition is reviewing that flashcard at increasing intervals rather than repeatedly in one sitting. An app like Anki or a Leitner box both apply spaced repetition to plain flashcards.

### How long before you notice spaced repetition working on English vocabulary?

Effects appear within weeks rather than months. The 74-learner classroom study behind this post ran for two full academic semesters, but the underlying spacing effect itself, better recall from spreading out review, shows up in single-session lab experiments as well as long field studies.
