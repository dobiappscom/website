# How to Find Your CEFR English Level (A1-C2)

*By [The Dobi Apps Team](https://dobiapps.com/authors/editorial) · Published 2026-09-16 · Originally published at [dobiapps.com](https://dobiapps.com/blog/find-your-cefr-english-level)*

> **EF SET's own validation study found its free CEFR test correlates moderately strongly with TOEFL iBT scores. How to test or self-assess your English level.**

**In short:** Finding your CEFR English level means either taking a free adaptive test, such as EF SET, which a 384-person validation study found correlates moderately strongly with TOEFL iBT reading and listening scores, or self-assessing against the Council of Europe's can-do descriptors. Either method beats guessing, and it determines which A1-C2 book or audiobook actually fits.

![A sample EF SET English Certificate showing a test score mapped to a CEFR level from A1 to C2](https://a.storyblok.com/f/79503/2856x1440/42a2bda166/ef-set-certificate.jpg)

*Certificate image: [EF SET](https://www.efset.org/)*

## What Is a CEFR Level, and Why Find Yours Before You Choose Reading or Listening Material?

A CEFR level is a score from A1 to C2 on the Common European Framework of
Reference for Languages, the six-band scale the Council of Europe designed to
describe what a language learner can actually do in listening, reading,
speaking, and writing, rather than how many years they studied. English exams
including IELTS, TOEFL, and Cambridge English report results on this same
scale, and EF SET states that its own results are "fully aligned with CEFR
levels" from beginner (A1) to proficient (C2).

Finding a CEFR level before picking reading or listening material matters
because the scale is also how graded readers, leveled audiobooks, and app
libraries organize their catalogs. A book labeled B1 assumes a reader who can
already handle "the main points of clear standard speech on familiar
matters," in the CEFR's own wording for that level, not a reader who is still
working out basic phrases. Picking material without knowing that starting
point means guessing at every purchase or download, and guessing produces two
failure modes: text far above a level, which collapses into constant
dictionary lookups, and text far below it, which teaches nothing new.

A CEFR level is also not a single number. A learner's reading level and
listening level frequently sit a band apart, since the two skills are built
differently and fade at different rates when unused. The rest of this guide
covers how to find each one, through a free test, a self-assessment
checklist, or a practical reading-behavior check, and what to do with the
answer once you have it.

## What Are the Six CEFR Levels, From A1 to C2?

The CEFR defines six levels, grouped in three pairs the Council of Europe
labels Basic User (A1-A2), Independent User (B1-B2), and Proficient User
(C1-C2). Each level carries its own "can-do" descriptors for listening,
reading, spoken interaction, spoken production, and writing, published
together in the Council of Europe's self-assessment grid. The descriptors
describe real tasks a learner can complete, not test scores or class hours,
which is what makes the scale usable for self-assessment rather than only for
exam grading.

The table below quotes the grid's own listening and reading descriptors for
every level. Match your own experience against each row, not against the
level label alone. A learner who understands "phrases and the highest
frequency vocabulary related to areas of most immediate personal relevance"
(A2) but has never tested the paragraph at C1 has not placed themselves yet;
they have only ruled out one end of the scale.

| CEFR level | Listening: "I can..." | Reading: "I can..." |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | understand familiar words and very basic phrases... when people speak slowly and clearly | understand familiar names, words and very simple sentences, for example on notices and posters |
| A2 | understand phrases and the highest frequency vocabulary related to areas of most immediate personal relevance... catch the main point in short, clear, simple messages | read very short, simple texts and find specific, predictable information in everyday material such as advertisements and menus |
| B1 | understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters... when the delivery is relatively slow and clear | understand texts that consist mainly of high frequency everyday or job-related language |
| B2 | understand extended speech and lectures and follow even complex lines of argument, provided the topic is reasonably familiar | read articles and reports concerned with contemporary problems, and understand contemporary literary prose |
| C1 | understand extended speech even when it is not clearly structured and when relationships are only implied and not signalled explicitly | understand long and complex factual and literary texts, appreciating distinctions of style |
| C2 | understand any kind of spoken language... even when delivered at fast native speed | read with ease virtually all forms of the written language, including abstract, structurally or linguistically complex texts |

Descriptors quoted from the [Council of Europe and Europass self-assessment
grid](https://europass.europa.eu/system/files/2020-05/CEFR%20self-assessment%20grid%20EN.pdf).

## Which Free Tests Can Tell You Your CEFR Level?

A free adaptive test is the fastest way to get a CEFR result you did not
calculate yourself. EF SET is one of the most widely used: it is free, timed
at either 50 minutes for reading and listening or 90 minutes for all four
skills, and EF states its "results [are] fully aligned with CEFR levels" from
A1 through C2.

> **30M+** — English tests taken on EF SET since 2016, a free test whose scores are reported directly on the CEFR A1-C2 scale
>
> *Source: [EF SET](https://www.efset.org/)*

The test is adaptive rather than fixed-length. EF SET's own FAQ describes an
engine where "the content of an individual's test is responsive to the level
of language proficiency the person demonstrates as the test unfolds," routing
a test-taker toward harder questions after correct answers and easier ones
after incorrect ones. That design is what lets a single test session, rather
than six separate ones, place both a true beginner and a near-native speaker
on the same scale in comparable time.

EF SET is not the only free option. The British Council's English Online
test and Strømmen's short placement test both report a CEFR result at no
cost and without an account, and either works as a faster, rougher first
pass before a longer test or a self-assessment checklist. None of the three
replaces a proctored exam like IELTS or TOEFL for a visa or university
application, a distinction covered next.

## How Reliable Are Free CEFR Placement Tests?

Free placement tests vary a lot in how carefully they were built, and one of
them has published a direct comparison against a paid, proctored exam. EF
SET's own validation study recruited 384 volunteer test-takers who had also
taken the TOEFL iBT, then compared scores on both tests.

> **0.70 / 0.77** — disattenuated correlation between EF SET Reading and TOEFL iBT Reading scores, and between EF SET Listening and TOEFL iBT Listening scores, from a 384-examinee validation study
>
> *Source: [EF SET PLUS-TOEFL iBT Correlation Study Report](https://a.storyblok.com/f/79503/x/8f28ee8ebc/ef-set-toefl-correlation-study-report.pdf)*

The report's own authors describe the result as "moderately strong, positive
correlations between EF SET PLUS and TOEFL for both the reading and listening
scales," which it calls "fairly solid convergent validity evidence," not
proof the two tests are interchangeable. The same report gives EF SET's
marginal reliability, a measure of how consistent a score is across repeated
administration, as 0.949 for reading and 0.944 for listening, based on a
sample of more than 37,000 test-takers, which the authors attribute to the
test's adaptive design.

This is industry-reported evidence: EF, the company that sells EF SET, ran
and published the study, not an independent journal. The report is candid
about the limit that follows. With only 384 examinees, it states that "any
statistical concordance would not be psychometrically appropriate," meaning a
score of 70 on EF SET should not be read as an exact stand-in for a specific
TOEFL number. Treat a free test's CEFR result as a strong starting estimate,
not a certified score, and pair it with the self-assessment checklist below.

## How Do You Self-Assess Your CEFR Level Without a Test?

Self-assessment against the same can-do grid works without any test at all,
and costs nothing but a few minutes of honest reading. Start at the Reading
row for A1 in the table above and read down, level by level, asking one
question at each row: does this describe something you can already do, right
now, in English, not something you are working toward? The last level where
the answer is still an unqualified yes, rather than the first level where it
becomes no, is the more reliable estimate, since learners consistently
overestimate rather than underestimate their own ability on a single
read-through.

Do the same exercise separately for listening. A learner who reads news
articles comfortably at B2 but still needs a speaker to slow down and
repeat, the A1 listening descriptor's own condition, has a reading level a
full three bands ahead of a listening level. That gap is common rather than
unusual, because reading exposes a learner to far more total words than
listening does in most self-study routines.

Self-assessment against can-do statements will not produce the numeric
precision of an adaptive test's score, and it is more vulnerable to a
learner's own optimism. Its advantage is that it costs nothing and can be
redone in minutes every time a learner suspects their level has moved, which
is the subject of the last section of this guide.

## How Many Unknown Words Per Page Signal the Right Reading Level?

A page-level check catches what a can-do statement cannot: whether a specific
book, not a hypothetical skill, actually fits. The Extensive Reading
Foundation recommends opening a book in the middle, not at the first page,
and reading a few minutes of it while asking three questions: can you read it
without a dictionary, are you reading at a natural pace, and do you
understand almost everything. A "no" to any of the three means the book sits
above the level to try right now.

Classroom reading research has tried to attach exact numbers to the same idea
for decades, most famously the reading specialist Emmett Betts' 1946 criteria
of 95 to 98 percent word-recognition accuracy for a text at a learner's
"instructional level," figures still printed in reading guides today.
Timothy Shanahan, Distinguished Professor Emeritus at the University of
Illinois Chicago and a past co-leader of the National Reading Panel, has been
blunt about where those specific percentages came from:

> “Unfortunately, there was no such study of learning. Betts just made up the numbers and teachers and professors have rapturously clung to them ever since.”
>
> — [Timothy Shanahan](https://www.shanahanonliteracy.com/blog/the-instructional-level-concept-revisited-teaching-with-complex-text-1), Distinguished Professor Emeritus, University of Illinois Chicago

That does not make the 95-to-98-percent range useless. It makes it a rule of
thumb rather than a measured constant, worth treating the same way as the
Extensive Reading Foundation's three-question test: a fast, practical filter,
not a certified score. In practice, more than one or two words per ordinary
page that send you to a dictionary is a reasonable trigger to try a book one
level down, whichever CEFR band your test or self-assessment placed you in.

## How Should Reading and Listening Behavior Change at Each CEFR Level?

Reading and listening behavior at a given CEFR level are consistent with
each other, which is a second way to sanity-check a result from a test or
checklist. A B1 listener, per the CEFR's own descriptor, needs delivery that
is "relatively slow and clear," which is why [does audiobook playback speed
affect English
comprehension](https://dobiapps.com/blog/audiobook-listening-speed-english-comprehension)
recommends starting B1 audio around 0.75x to 0.85x rather than a narrator's
default 1.0x. A C2 listener, by contrast, has "no difficulty... even when
delivered at fast native speed," so slowing audio down at that level trades
away practice for comfort rather than adding comprehension.

Reading behavior follows the same logic through vocabulary coverage rather
than playback speed. [Comprehensible input and finding your right English
reading level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/comprehensible-input-english-reading-level) covers a
controlled study that found comprehension dropped sharply once unfamiliar
words passed a specific density threshold, the same mechanism behind the
page test in the section above: too many unfamiliar words per page and the
guess-from-context process that builds vocabulary stops working.

A learner whose CEFR test result and reading-behavior check disagree by more
than one level should trust the behavior check first. A test result is a
snapshot from one sitting; how much of an actual page or chapter you
understand unassisted, this week, on the material you are about to spend
time with, is the more immediate signal for what to read or listen to next.

## How Often Should You Re-Check Your CEFR Level?

A CEFR level is worth re-checking whenever the page test from earlier in
this guide stops flagging anything. Once a book at your current level rarely
produces a word you need to look up, across more than one title rather than
one lucky chapter, that is the practical signal to try the page test again
one level up, the same signal covered in [comprehensible input and finding
your right English reading level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/comprehensible-input-english-reading-level).

A fixed schedule works as a backup for a learner who would rather not rely on
noticing the signal. Re-running a free test such as EF SET, or re-reading the
can-do grid against your own reading and listening this week, every two to
three months catches a level change neither too early, before real progress
has accumulated, nor so late that a learner spends months on material that
has become too easy. Whichever cadence you use, the outcome that matters is
the one this guide started with: a level that determines which book or
audiobook you actually pick up next, not a number filed away and forgotten.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a free online CEFR test as accurate as IELTS or TOEFL?

Not for official purposes, though the evidence is closer than you might expect. EF SET's own validation study found its Reading and Listening scores correlated moderately strongly with TOEFL iBT scores from the same 384 test-takers, which the study's authors call solid convergent validity evidence rather than proof the two are interchangeable. A free test is reliable enough to choose reading and listening material by; a university or visa application still requires a proctored exam such as IELTS, TOEFL, or Cambridge English.

### What if my reading level and listening level are different CEFR levels?

Treat them separately rather than picking one label for both. It is common, and the CEFR's own self-assessment grid is built with separate rows for listening and reading for exactly this reason. Pick reading material at your reading-level result and audio at your listening-level result, even when the two numbers do not match, rather than downgrading the stronger skill to match the weaker one.
