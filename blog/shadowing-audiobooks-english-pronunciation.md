# Does Shadowing Audiobooks Improve English Pronunciation?

*By [The Dobi Apps Team](https://dobiapps.com/authors/editorial) · Published 2026-09-11 · Originally published at [dobiapps.com](https://dobiapps.com/blog/shadowing-audiobooks-english-pronunciation)*

> **Shadowing means speaking along with audio in real time. A 2025 study found it raised EFL listening scores 32% in 8 weeks and linked it to pronunciation gains.**

**In short:** Shadowing means speaking an audiobook's words out loud at the same time as the narrator, matching rhythm and intonation rather than repeating afterward. A 2025 study of 30 EFL students found shadowing raised listening scores by 32% over eight weeks, and a separate controlled study found significant pronunciation and intonation gains from shadowing compared to a non-shadowing group.

## What Is the Shadowing Technique in Language Learning?

Shadowing is speaking a piece of audio out loud in real time, as closely
behind the narrator's voice as possible, rather than listening first and
repeating afterward. A learner tracks the words as they arrive and reproduces
them immediately, which is different from simple repetition after a pause.

The technique was originally developed to train interpreters, who need to
listen and speak at the same time under pressure, before language teachers
adapted it for general pronunciation and listening practice. A 2025 study
published in LEARN Journal describes the mechanism this way: shadowing
"involves auditory input, speech output, repetition, divided attention, and
engages the working-memory system" all at once, which is what separates it
from passive listening or read-aloud practice done from a transcript.

Because shadowing forces a learner to produce sounds at the narrator's actual
speaking pace, it exposes gaps that slower, self-paced repetition hides:
where a stress pattern falls in a sentence, how one word runs into the next,
and where a native speaker's intonation rises or falls. Those are exactly the
features a learner who only reads a transcript silently never has to
reproduce, and a different set of gaps than the vocabulary-focused practice
covered in [how spaced repetition builds English vocabulary
retention](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary).

## Does Research Show Shadowing Improves English Pronunciation?

Two 2025 studies measured shadowing's effect with pre-test and post-test
designs, one on listening comprehension and one on pronunciation directly,
and both found statistically significant gains.

> **32%** — increase in listening comprehension score after 8 weeks of shadowing practice, from a mean of 12.1 to 15.98 out of 16 questions, among 30 Chinese EFL high school students (t = -5.993, p < .001)
>
> *Source: [Mu & Wasuntarasophit, 2025](https://doi.org/10.70730/SFKP2045)*

That study, by Yating Mu and Sukhum Wasuntarasophit at Khon Kaen University,
measured listening comprehension as its primary outcome rather than
pronunciation directly, using a paired-sample t-test on identical pre- and
post-listening tests. Its own questionnaire data also recorded students'
perceptions of improved pronunciation and intonation, though that part is
self-reported rather than independently scored.

A separate 2025 controlled study by María Angélica Rivadeneira Zambrano,
Jardel Coutinho dos Santos, and Gabriela Gáleas Arboleda, published in Forum
for Linguistic Studies, tested pronunciation directly. Thirty-nine
pre-service teachers were split into a shadowing group and a control group
and scored before and after on a rubric covering intelligibility,
articulation of individual sounds, word and sentence stress, rhythm, and
intonation. The study reports "statistically significant improvements in all
phonological aspects among the experimental group, surpassing those observed
in the control group," which is qualitative confirmation of an effect rather
than a specific reported percentage. Together, the two studies point the same
direction from two different angles: shadowing measurably improves listening
comprehension, and a version of the same technique measurably improves
pronunciation when scored against a rubric.

## How Does Shadowing Audiobooks Differ From Reading While Listening?

Shadowing and reading while listening both pair a learner with narrated
audio, but they train different skills. Reading while listening keeps a
learner's eyes on the printed page while the audio plays, with no speaking
involved, which is why [reading while listening to audiobooks improves
English listening fluency and vocabulary
retention](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency) rather than
pronunciation specifically. Shadowing removes the requirement to look at text
at all and adds the requirement to speak, which shifts the practice from
input processing toward output production.

A learner does not have to choose one permanently. Reading while listening
works well for a first pass through a new audiobook chapter, at a
comprehensible input level where most of the vocabulary is already familiar,
covered in [how to find the right English reading
level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/comprehensible-input-english-reading-level). Shadowing works
well as a second pass through the same short passage, once the content is
familiar enough that a learner's attention can shift from figuring out
meaning to reproducing sound.

Playback speed is a third, separate lever on the same audio. Slowing a
passage down before shadowing it, rather than changing what a learner does
with the audio, changes how fast it plays, and the research on [whether
audiobook playback speed affects English listening
comprehension](https://dobiapps.com/blog/audiobook-listening-speed-english-comprehension) points
to slowing a chapter down first and shadowing it once its content and pace
are both manageable, rather than shadowing a passage at native speed before
either one is.

## How Do You Shadow an Audiobook Step by Step?

Start with a passage short enough to hold in working memory, 30 seconds to a
minute of audio, rather than shadowing an entire chapter in one attempt.
Listen to it once with no speaking, so the content and vocabulary are no
longer new. This first pass matters because shadowing something incomprehensible
just produces mimicked noise, not language practice.

On the second listen, speak the words out loud at the same time as the
narrator, staying as close behind their pace as possible rather than pausing
to think. Do not aim for word-perfect accuracy on the first attempt. The
goal on an early pass is matching rhythm and intonation, catching up on
individual words is secondary and improves with repetition.

Repeat the same short passage three or four times before moving to the next
one. Each repetition frees up attention that the previous pass spent on
simply keeping pace, which is what lets a learner notice smaller details,
like where a word's stress falls, on the third or fourth attempt that were
impossible to catch on the first.

## What Makes an Audiobook Good for Shadowing Practice?

An audiobook chosen for shadowing needs to sit close to a learner's
comprehension level, for the same reason covered in [comprehensible input and
finding your right English reading
level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/comprehensible-input-english-reading-level): material that is
mostly incomprehensible gives nothing accurate to imitate, and a learner ends
up mimicking sounds without understanding what they are producing.

## How Often Should You Practice Shadowing to See Results?

The 2025 listening comprehension study measured gains after eight weeks of
twice-weekly 45-minute sessions, a total of 12 hours of shadowing practice
spread across the study period. That is a realistic minimum to expect
results by, not a one-off exercise that produces a noticeable change after a
single session.

Short, frequent sessions outperform occasional long ones for the same reason
spaced repetition beats cramming for vocabulary, covered in [how spaced
repetition builds English vocabulary
retention](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary): a skill built on
motor memory and rhythm, which pronunciation is, consolidates through
repeated practice over time rather than through a single long session. Ten
to fifteen minutes of shadowing a few times a week, applied to audio a
learner already finds comprehensible, matches the pacing both 2025 studies
used to produce a measurable result.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is shadowing the same as reading while listening?

No. Reading while listening means following printed text with your eyes while audio plays, with no speaking involved. Shadowing means speaking the words out loud, at the same time as the narrator, without necessarily looking at any text at all.

### Do you need to understand every word to shadow an audiobook?

No, but the audio should be close to your comprehension level. Shadowing trades comprehension effort for pronunciation and rhythm practice, so audio several levels above what you can follow leaves nothing to imitate accurately.
