# Does Reading While Listening to Audiobooks Improve English?

*By [The Dobi Apps Team](https://dobiapps.com/authors/editorial) · Published 2026-09-10 · Originally published at [dobiapps.com](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency)*

> **A 26-week EFL study found reading while listening to audiobooks lifted English listening scores and produced 4x the vocabulary gain of a control group.**

**In short:** Reading while listening to an audiobook improves English listening fluency and vocabulary more than listening alone. In a 26-week study of EFL learners, the reading-while-listening group improved dictation scores by more than 100% and gained over four times as many vocabulary words as a control group taught with regular instruction alone.

## What Is Reading While Listening?

Reading while listening (RWL) means following the printed text of a story
at the same time as its narrated audio, rather than listening with no text
or reading with no audio. The method is distinct from the reverse pairing
used in first-language reading instruction, "listening while reading"
(LWR), where reading is the goal and the audio simply moves faster to keep
pace with a fluent reader.

In reading while listening, listening is the goal. The written text exists
to help a learner match sounds to words they already recognize on the page,
build a sense of the language's natural rhythm and chunking, and avoid the
word-by-word decoding that slows down a beginner's reading anyway. A 2011
study in the Asian Journal of English Language Teaching by Anna
Ching-Shyang Chang describes RWL as help for learners to "match the spoken
form with the written form to develop the skills of auditory
discrimination and word recognition" ([Chang,
2011](https://www.dyslexicadvantage.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Reading-While-Listening.pdf)).

Because RWL asks less of a learner than listening with no text at all, it
is documented as particularly useful for learners whose listening
proficiency is still low, the same learners who benefit from picking
[stories at the right reading
level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) rather than unabridged native
material.

## Does Reading While Listening Improve English Listening Fluency?

Chang's 2011 study followed 19 EFL learners of comparable English
proficiency over 26 weeks, two semesters of 13 weeks each. Seven students
volunteered for the reading-while-listening treatment; the other 12
received regular classroom instruction as a control group. Both groups
took the same 80-item listening test, 40 multiple-choice questions and 40
dictation items, delivered at a natural speech rate of 160 words per
minute, before and after the intervention.

> **100%+** — improvement in dictation scores for the reading-while-listening group after 26 weeks, versus the control group taught with regular instruction alone
>
> *Source: [Chang, 2011, Asian Journal of English Language Teaching](https://www.dyslexicadvantage.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Reading-While-Listening.pdf)*

Chang reports that the reading-while-listening group "improved more than
100% on dictation scores, implying that RWL increased students' speed in
the listening process." Dictation, writing down exactly what is heard, is a
direct test of whether a learner can process spoken English at
conversational speed rather than needing it slowed down, so a gain that
large points at a real fluency improvement rather than a familiarity effect
from the same test appearing twice.

## How Much English Vocabulary Do Learners Gain From Reading While Listening?

The same study measured vocabulary with a pre- and post-intervention
vocabulary levels test. The reading-while-listening group gained 17 marks
on that test, equivalent to about 566 individual words. The control group,
taught with regular instruction and no RWL component, gained 4 marks, about
123 words, over the same 26 weeks.

That is more than four times the vocabulary gain from a method whose
stated purpose was listening fluency, not vocabulary teaching. Chang
attributes part of the gap to reading volume: the treatment group read 86
books in the first 13 weeks and 156 books in the second 13 weeks, an 81%
increase in quantity, and moved on to longer, more difficult books as the
study progressed. More exposure to English text, at a pace a learner could
follow because the audio was doing part of the decoding work, produced more
opportunities to meet new words in context, the same mechanism that makes
[extensive reading effective for vocabulary
building](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) on its own.

## How Does Reading While Listening Compare to Listening Only or Reading Only?

Separate research cited in Chang's review points the same direction. Brown,
Waring, and Donkaewbua (2008) compared three modes, reading only, reading
while listening, and listening only, with Japanese college students working
through graded readers, and found students learned the most new words in
the reading-while-listening mode. Chang's own 2009 study of Taiwanese
college students found a comprehension score about 10% higher in the
reading-while-listening mode than in listening only, on two short stories
of matched length and difficulty.

| Study | Modes compared | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chang (2011), 26-week EFL study | Reading while listening vs. regular instruction | RWL group: 100%+ gain in dictation scores, 566 words gained vs. 123 for control |
| Brown, Waring & Donkaewbua (2008) | Reading only vs. reading while listening vs. listening only | Most vocabulary learned in reading-while-listening mode |
| Chang (2009) | Reading while listening vs. listening only | About 10% higher comprehension score in reading-while-listening mode |

Learner preference lines up with the outcomes. In a separate small-scale
study Chang cites, 58% of students preferred reading while listening, 40%
preferred reading only, and 2% preferred listening only, with students
reporting that the paired mode made stories easier to follow, more
interesting, and easier to concentrate on.

## How Should You Start Using Audiobooks Alongside Reading?

Start with a story at a reading level that is already comfortable rather
than a difficult one, since the research above measured RWL against
regular graded readers, not against material a learner is struggling to
parse even without audio. [Choosing a story at the right
level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) matters as much for RWL as it
does for reading alone.

Play the audio at a natural pace rather than slowed down. The point of RWL
is exposure to real speech rate, and slowing it down removes the fluency
benefit the studies above measured. [Dobi Apps' Audiobook](https://dobiapps.com/audiobook)
pairs narrated audio with synchronized text across CEFR levels A1 through
C2, which lets a learner move from picture-book pacing to a natural adult
reading speed as listening fluency builds.

New words encountered this way are still worth reviewing deliberately
afterward. Reading while listening builds fluency and exposure; locking a
specific new word into long-term memory is a separate step, covered in [how
spaced repetition builds English vocabulary
retention](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary).

## When Should You Stop Reading and Listening at the Same Time?

Chang's review notes that RWL is most valuable for learners whose listening
proficiency is still developing, and that the field's broader guidance is
to wean learners away from simultaneous reading and listening once
proficiency rises, moving them toward independent reading or independent
listening. The skill an intermediate-to-advanced learner actually needs,
understanding spoken English with no text in front of them, is not the
skill RWL practices.

A practical marker: once a learner can follow a graded audiobook at natural
speed with the text covered and still answer basic comprehension questions
correctly, independent listening practice will do more for real-world
listening than continuing to read along. Until that point, the studies
above show reading while listening outperforming both reading only and
listening only for building the fluency that gets a learner there, the same
extensive input that [makes learning English through stories work in the
first place](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories). A learner who has reached
that marker and wants to work on speaking next, rather than only listening,
can apply the same audiobooks to [shadowing practice for English
pronunciation](https://dobiapps.com/blog/shadowing-audiobooks-english-pronunciation), which adds
speaking the words aloud instead of only following them silently.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is reading while listening the same as listening only?

No. Reading while listening (RWL) shows the written text at the same time as the audio, so a learner matches the spoken form to the written form. Listening only (LO) gives no text, which is closer to a real conversation but harder for a learner who is still building listening fluency.

### Should advanced English learners keep reading while listening?

Not indefinitely. Reading while listening is documented as most useful for learners who are still building listening fluency. Once comprehension is solid, weaning off simultaneous reading and listening toward independent listening builds the skill a learner actually needs in conversation.
