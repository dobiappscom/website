## What Are the Top 5 Apps for Learning English in 2026?

The five apps worth trying to learn English in 2026 each target a different
skill rather than competing on the same one: [Storybook](https://dobiapps.com/storybook) for
reading and vocabulary through stories, Duolingo for daily habit-building,
ELSA Speak for pronunciation, HelloTalk for conversation with native
speakers, and Anki for long-term vocabulary retention. No single app on this
list replaces the other four; a learner who wants real fluency typically
needs at least two.

| App | Best for | Notable data point | Cost |

## Which App Is Best for Reading and Building English Vocabulary Through Stories?

[Storybook](https://dobiapps.com/storybook), from Dobi Apps, is the strongest pick for a learner
who wants to build English vocabulary the way [extensive
reading](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) is documented to work: through
repeated exposure to words inside a story rather than an isolated word list.
Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/story-book-english/id6498920336)
counts more than 1,000,000 free books, comics, and audiobooks, organized by
level, plus reading games and progress tracking, the kind of reward system
covered in [do English learning games improve
vocabulary](https://dobiapps.com/blog/do-english-learning-games-improve-vocabulary), which looks
at what separates a game mechanic that builds real vocabulary from one that
only tracks time in the app. Its comics library in particular gives
beginners and reluctant readers a research-backed alternative to text-only
stories, covered in [do comics and graphic novels help you learn
English](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learning-english-through-comics-graphic-novels).

Storybook's library spans the full range from picture books for beginners to
unabridged material for advanced readers, which matters because [choosing a
story at the right level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) is what makes
extensive reading effective in the first place. A learner who
wants a matching audiobook to practice pronunciation while reading can pair
Storybook with [Audiobook](https://dobiapps.com/audiobook), Dobi Apps' companion app for leveled
English audiobooks from A1 to C2, for the same [reading-while-listening
approach that improved English listening
scores](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency) in a controlled
study.

Storybook is free with no subscription, which sets it apart from most of the
other apps on this list, all of which reserve their fuller feature sets for
a paid tier.

## Which App Is Best for Building a Daily English Learning Habit?

Duolingo is the best fit for a learner whose main obstacle is consistency
rather than access to content. Its lessons run a few minutes each, and
streak tracking and daily reminders are built around getting a learner to
open the app again the next day rather than around depth in any single
lesson.

> **154 countries** — where English is Duolingo's #1 most-studied language, up 14% over 2024
>
> *Source: [Duolingo, 2025 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/)*

Duolingo's own [2025 Language
Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/) states
that "English learning soared again, becoming the #1 language in 154
countries," and that English is now the most-studied language in 79% of all
countries on the platform. Duolingo does not break out its total user base by
course, but its [Q2 FY2026 shareholder
letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)
reported 140.6 million monthly active users for the quarter ended June 30,
2026, up 10% year over year, so a large share of that base is very likely
studying English specifically.

The trade-off is depth of speaking practice. Duolingo's exercises are mostly
multiple choice, word banks, and short recorded phrases, which build
recognition faster than they build the unscripted, back-and-forth speaking a
learner needs for a real conversation.

## Which App Is Best for English Pronunciation and Speaking Practice?

ELSA Speak is the strongest choice for a learner working specifically on
pronunciation. The app uses AI speech recognition to score a spoken phrase
sound by sound and flag which specific phoneme, not just which word, a
learner mispronounced, then gives a targeted drill for that sound.

A 2026 quasi-experimental study of 39 vocational high school students in
Indonesia measured pronunciation scores before and after four weeks of
practice with ELSA Speak, finding an increase from a mean of 73.08 in the
pre-test to 81.56 in the post-test, a statistically significant gain
(p < 0.001) confirmed by a paired sample t-test ([Navia & Fediyanto,
2026](https://jele.or.id/index.php/jele/article/view/2373)). The study
covered one class at one school with no separate control group, so the
result is best read as evidence that the app can help within a classroom
setting, not as proof of a fixed score gain for every learner.

> “"AI-based applications such as ELSA Speak can serve as a viable supplementary tool in the EFL classroom, particularly for enhancing students' pronunciation through autonomous and technology-assisted practice."”
>
> — [Navia & Fediyanto, 2026](https://jele.or.id/index.php/jele/article/view/2373), Journal of English Language and Education

ELSA Speak works best as a supplement a learner uses for short, focused
pronunciation drills alongside a broader English learning routine, rather
than as the only app in that routine.

## Which App Is Best for Practicing English Conversation With Native Speakers?

HelloTalk is the best option for a learner who wants unscripted conversation
with a real native English speaker rather than a scripted exercise or an AI
tutor. The app matches learners who want to practice each other's languages,
so an English learner typically pairs with a native English speaker who is
learning the English learner's own language in return.

HelloTalk [reports](https://www.hellotalk.com/en/about) more than 70 million
users across 200-plus countries and support for over 260 languages, which
gives an English learner a wide pool of potential language partners to
message, voice call, or video call at any hour rather than during a
scheduled class. Built-in correction tools let a native speaker mark up a
learner's message directly, and a translation feature lowers the barrier to
a first conversation.

Because HelloTalk connects a learner with strangers, the same caution that
applies to any messaging platform applies here: keep early conversations to
the in-app chat, and treat a request to move to another platform or app
immediately as a reason for caution rather than urgency.

## Which App Is Best for Long-Term English Vocabulary Retention?

Anki is the best tool for making new English vocabulary stick once a
learner has already met it in reading, a class, or a conversation. Rather
than reviewing every flashcard on a fixed daily schedule, Anki schedules
each card's next review individually, using either the classic SM-2
algorithm or the newer FSRS algorithm, [both of which ship in current
versions of the app](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm).

This scheduling approach is the same [spaced repetition
principle](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary) documented in a 2024
meta-analysis of 48 experiments and 3,411 learners, which found spaced
practice produces a medium-to-large effect on second-language vocabulary
retention compared to massed study ([Lafleur & Kanazawa,
2024](https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/download/87/396/1359)).
Anki is not English-specific: a learner builds or downloads their own deck
of English words rather than following a built-in course, which makes it a
companion to the other four apps rather than a full replacement for any of
them.

Anki is free on Android, desktop, and the web. The official iOS app,
[AnkiMobile](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ankimobile-flashcards/id373493387),
is a $24.99 one-time purchase that funds the project instead of running on
subscriptions or ads.

## How Should You Combine These Apps to Learn English Faster?

None of these five apps covers every skill an English learner needs, so
combining two or three that target different skills works better than
relying on one alone. A learner building vocabulary through [Storybook's
leveled stories](https://dobiapps.com/storybook) meets a new word in context; reviewing that
same word in [Anki on a schedule timed to when it is about to be
forgotten](https://dobiapps.com/blog/spaced-repetition-english-vocabulary) is what keeps it from
fading a few weeks later.

The same layering applies to speaking. Duolingo builds recognition of
grammar and core vocabulary through short daily lessons, ELSA Speak
sharpens the pronunciation of individual sounds once a learner has
something to say, and HelloTalk supplies the unscripted, back-and-forth
conversation that neither an app running exercises nor an AI feedback loop
can fully substitute for.

A learner short on time gets more from two apps used consistently, one for
input and one for output, than from all five apps used for a few days each
before being abandoned.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Duolingo enough on its own to become fluent in English?

No. Duolingo builds vocabulary and grammar through short daily lessons, but its exercises give a learner little unscripted speaking practice. Pairing it with a pronunciation app such as ELSA Speak or a conversation app such as HelloTalk covers the speaking practice Duolingo's format does not.

### Which of these five English learning apps are free?

Storybook, Duolingo, HelloTalk, and ELSA Speak are all free to download with an optional paid tier for extra features. Anki is free on Android, desktop, and the web; the official iOS app, AnkiMobile, is a one-time $24.99 purchase that funds the project's development.

### What is the best app for a complete beginner in English?

Storybook or Duolingo both work well for a true beginner. Storybook suits a learner, including a child, who wants to build vocabulary through leveled stories, comics, and audiobooks. Duolingo suits a learner who wants short, structured, gamified lessons in grammar and core vocabulary.
