# Audiobook or Ebook App: Which Fits Your English Goal?

*By [The Dobi Apps Team](https://dobiapps.com/authors/editorial) · Published 2026-09-11 · Updated 2026-09-14 · Originally published at [dobiapps.com](https://dobiapps.com/blog/audiobook-or-ebook-app-for-learning-english)*

> **A 186-learner EFL study found text-audio produced the largest listening gains and text-picture the largest reading gains, but all three combined added nothing.**

**In short:** Whether to pick an audiobook app or an ebook app for English depends on the specific skill being built. A study of 186 EFL learners found the text-plus-audio format produced the largest listening comprehension gains and the text-plus-picture format produced the largest reading gains, while combining text, audio, and pictures together added no further benefit over either simpler pairing.

## What's the Difference Between an Audiobook App and an Ebook App for English?

An audiobook app for English centers on narrated speech: a learner presses
play and follows spoken English, sometimes with synchronized text running
alongside the audio. An ebook app centers on the written page: books,
comics, or graded readers a learner reads at their own pace, sometimes with
an optional narration track added on top. The two formats overlap once
narration and text appear together, but they start from different defaults,
audio-first versus text-first, and that default shapes which skill the app
builds fastest.

Neither default is automatically the right choice. A learner working on
[reading English through stories](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) at
the right level needs a text-first library to browse and to skim before
committing to a book, which favors an ebook app's structure. A learner whose
main gap is understanding spoken English at natural speed needs regular
narrated input, which favors an audiobook app's structure. Picking the
format that matches the actual skill gap, rather than picking whichever app
has more features listed on its store page, is the decision this post is
built to help with.

## Does Pairing Text With Audio Build English Skills Faster Than Either Alone?

Pairing text with audio produced the largest listening comprehension gain
of any single format tested in a 2021 study of 186 Korean EFL learners,
and the second-largest reading gain, ahead of using text or audio alone
for either skill. Audio alone, with no text, produced no statistically
significant gain in either listening or reading over five weeks.

Na-Young Kim's study, published in the Journal of English Teaching through
Movies and Media, split 186 college students into six groups: text-only,
audio-only, text-picture, audio-picture, text-audio, and text-audio-picture.
Each group studied the same fairy tales in its assigned format for five
weeks, with a TOEIC-based listening and reading test given before and after.

> **12.55** — listening posttest score for the text-audio group, the highest of any format tested, versus 7.22 for text-only and 8.58 for audio-only (p = .000)
>
> *Source: [Kim, 2021, Journal of English Teaching through Movies and Media](https://doi.org/10.16875/stem.2021.22.3.29)*

| Format | Listening posttest score | Listening gain significant? | Reading posttest score | Reading gain significant? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Text only | 7.22 | No (p = .259) | 9.72 | Yes (p = .001) |
| Audio only | 8.58 | No (p = .094) | 8.09 | No (p = .744) |
| Text + picture | 10.83 | Yes (p = .000) | 12.24 | Yes (p = .000) |
| Audio + picture | 10.72 | Yes (p = .000) | 7.09 | No (p = .074) |
| Text + audio | 12.55 | Yes (p = .000) | 11.83 | Yes (p = .000) |
| Text + audio + picture | 10.16 | No (p = .263) | 9.52 | No (p = .851) |

Text-audio is the only format in the table with a significant gain on both
listening and reading at once, which lines up with a separate 26-week study
covered in [how reading while listening to audiobooks builds English
listening fluency](https://dobiapps.com/blog/reading-while-listening-english-fluency): pairing
narrated audio with its matching text builds more than one skill from the
same session.

## Does Adding Pictures on Top of Text and Audio Help Even More?

No. The group that combined all three formats, text, audio, and pictures
together, showed no significant gain in either listening or reading, while
the simpler text-audio pairing and the simpler text-picture pairing each
produced a significant gain on their own skill. Stacking a third channel
onto an already-effective pairing did not add further comprehension in this
study.

Kim's own conclusion states the finding plainly: "Using more modalities did
not result in more comprehension in both listening and reading." The
text-audio-picture group scored 10.16 on the listening posttest (p = .263,
not significant) and 9.52 on the reading posttest (p = .851, not
significant), both lower than the best-performing two-format pairing on
each respective skill.

This result matters for a practical reason: an app that layers text,
narration, and illustration into a single screen is not automatically doing
more for comprehension than an app built around one well-matched pairing.
[Comics and graphic novels](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learning-english-through-comics-graphic-novels)
already demonstrate that a text-picture pairing alone measurably helps
reading comprehension, and adding a third, unrelated channel on top of an
already-working pairing is not the same as adding more benefit. The format
that matches the specific skill being built outperforms the format with the
most channels stacked together.

## Which Format Should You Pick for Your English Learning Goal?

Pick text-plus-audio when the goal is listening comprehension: it produced
the largest listening gain in Kim's study and a significant reading gain at
the same time, the only format tested that moved both scores together. Pick
text-plus-picture when the goal is reading comprehension specifically: it
produced the largest reading gain in the same study.

A learner using audio with no text at all should not expect a large gain
from that format alone. Audio-only was the format with no significant gain
on either measure in Kim's study, which suggests that narrated audio works
best in this research as a companion to text rather than as a stand-alone
input, a pattern also reported for advanced-beginner readers building
[comprehensible input at the right reading
level](https://dobiapps.com/blog/comprehensible-input-english-reading-level).

None of this means a single app has to cover every format. A learner who
already reads consistently can add a text-audio session for listening
practice without giving up the text-picture reading that is already working
for vocabulary, the same complementary pairing behind [why extensive
reading builds English vocabulary](https://dobiapps.com/blog/learn-english-through-stories) on
one side and listening fluency on the other. The specific skill gap, not
the app with the longest feature list, is what should decide the format.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an audio-only app enough to learn English, or is a reading app also needed?

Audio alone was the weakest format in the EFL study behind this post: the audio-only group's gain was not statistically significant for either listening or reading. Pairing audio with text, rather than using audio by itself, is what produced the largest listening score gain.

### Does adding pictures on top of text and audio make English learning even more effective?

Not according to the same study. The group that combined text, audio, and pictures together showed no significant gain in either listening or reading, while the simpler text-audio and text-picture pairings each produced a significant gain on their own skill.
